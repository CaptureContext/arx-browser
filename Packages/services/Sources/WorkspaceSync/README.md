# WorkspaceSync

iCloud sync of the SQLite workspace (DEV-560, phase 2a of ADR 0001), behind the `ARX_ICLOUD_SYNC` feature flag, on by default for the beta (DEV-1143); `0`, `false`, `no` or `off` turns it off. Sync itself still starts off, so the flag alone sends nothing. **Don't turn sync on for real data yet**, and never in a Release build before the Production schema is deployed (DEV-1207, below): deletions stay local (DEV-980), but the rest of ADR 0001's decisions of 15 November isn't built. With the flag off `WorkspaceSync.make` returns `nil`: no engine, no `CKContainer`, no files. It returns `nil` too when the build isn't entitled to the container (`ICloudEntitlement`), as a signed Arx Dev without its provisioning profile (DEV-1013), since CloudKit stops such a process. With the flag on, nothing reaches CloudKit until the person turns sync on.

- `WorkspaceSync` owns one SQLiteData `SyncEngine` and record zone per `SyncCategory`: Profiles and spaces (`organization`: `profile`, `space`, `folder`, `pinnedTab`, `favorite`, and DEV-980's `deletion`, `usage`, `syncDevice`) and Preferences (`preferences`: `preference`, `searchShortcut`, `keyboardShortcut`, `routingRule`, `proxyLinkRule`), in the private database of `iCloud.dev.capturecontext.arx`. Each turns off on its own, keeping the local and the cloud copy. Preferences and History pause: edits made meanwhile are sent on resume. Profiles and spaces and Tab Archive leave (DEV-1148, below).
- `WorkspaceSyncDelegate` never deletes local data. Sign-out pauses; signing in to the same account resumes; another account pauses until the person picks an `AccountDecision`: Use with This Account (forgets the old account's sync state and uploads every row, merging with what's there), Keep on This Mac (sync off), or Replace (copies every database to `arx-icloud-sync-backups/`, forgets the engine state, deletes the synced rows here without sending the deletions, then downloads the account's; the library's `deleteLocalData()` isn't used because it doesn't report a failed deletion).
- `WorkspaceRepair` is the deterministic repair pass, run once an engine finishes fetching (`SyncBatchObserver`) and after each start. A placement repair moves a folder or pinned tab only when iCloud has it and its parent where this Mac does (`CloudAccess.placements`), and breaks a cycle only when iCloud has the same one: a fetch can deliver a child a page before its parent, or fail in between. A tab in two places (a pin brought back beside its Favorite or beside this Mac's regular tab) keeps the Favorite or the regular tab; a pin beside a Favorite is removed only when iCloud has both records, since a fetch can stop halfway through a move between them. Preference repairs (duplicate keywords and shortcuts) likewise run only when those rows match iCloud (`CloudAccess.recordValues`). Anything iCloud doesn't confirm waits for a later pass.
- History (DEV-984, ADR 0001 decision 5) is the third category, `history`: `historyVisit` and `historyClear` in `arx-history.sqlite` (`HistoryDatabase`), made only when the history is in SQLite. It's off by default (`SyncCategory.isOnByDefault`, `SyncSettings.enabledCategories`) and mirrors `SyncSettings.historyWindowDays` of visits, 30 unless the person picks another. `WorkspaceSync` tells the history database the window while History is on (`HistoryDatabase.setMirrorWindow`), which mirrors every profile's visits from the window and prunes older mirror rows; the history store keeps the mirror from then on. There's no repair pass: `remoteChanges()` yields `.history`, and the app has `BrowserHistoryClient.applySynced` take the mirror and the clears into each profile's history. The mirror is separate from local history, so a pruned or deleted mirror row never removes a page from anyone's history. A shorter window on one Mac prunes the mirror for every Mac. "This Mac only" profiles (DEV-981) must be left out of the mirror when they're built.
- Tab Archive (DEV-983, ADR 0001 decision 4) is the fourth category, `archive`: `archiveEntry` with its own `archiveDeletion` and `archiveDevice` in `arx-archive.sqlite` (record types the organization's zone doesn't use, since SQLiteData routes fetched records by type), on the store's `archiveDatabase`. It's off by default, so archives stay on each Mac until the person turns it on. Turning it on marks the file (`ArchiveStateKey.deletionsStayLocal`): from then a restored entry is hidden on this Mac and stays on the others. Its repair pass (`WorkspaceRepair.repairArchive`) deletes an entry everywhere by the organization's rules, every Mac seen in 30 days removed it or "sync deletions after" passed, confirmed against iCloud's archive zone, with this Mac's device ID from the organization file. A batch reloads the workspace, as the organization's do. Replace with the Account's Data drops an archive change a save hadn't finished, so it can't bring the old account's entries back. A "this Mac only" profile's entries stay in the store's local file (DEV-981).
- "This Mac only" profiles (DEV-981) never sync: the store keeps them in `arx-organization-local.sqlite`, which no engine has, and leaves their synced copies as they are, with no deletion rows, so no purge can follow from turning a profile local. The repair pass never treats one as a duplicate starter profile, the history store neither mirrors nor takes in their history (`HistoryDatabase.setLocalProfiles`), and their Tab Archive entries stay in the local file.
- Preferences groups (DEV-982, ADR 0001 decision 3): appearance, search shortcuts, keyboard shortcuts and aliases, and routing, each a `SyncToggle` beside the categories'. A group turned off keeps this Mac's values in a `localPreference` override row (`PreferenceGroup`, in WorkspaceDatabase), which is the one record of the choice; saves leave its synced rows as they were, and other Macs' changes still arrive there without showing. Turning it on keeps this Mac's values or takes iCloud's. The app makes the change as a save (`BrowserWorkspaceClient.setPreferenceGroup`), then `refreshStatus()` publishes `WorkspaceSyncStatus.localPreferenceGroups`. `WorkspaceRepair.repairPreferences` skips a group kept local. The groups keep their choice while Preferences is off.
- The device directory (DEV-979): `arx-sync-devices.sqlite` (`DeviceDirectory`), with its own engine and `devices` zone outside `SyncCategory`, running while sync is on. Each Mac writes only its own `syncDeviceDirectory` row: name, last seen, the categories it syncs, the Preferences groups it keeps, `stoppedAt`, and its "sync deletions after" (`syncDeletionsAfterDays`, DEV-1337, `nil` while off). Settings lists the Macs under each category (`WorkspaceSyncStatus.devices(syncing:)`). `stopSyncingThisMac()` saves the stop (`SyncSettings.isStopped`), stops and drains every engine, then sends the stop to the directory, later if the account can't be reached now; the account stays connected. Purges leave out Macs that left the category (`DeletionPolicy.departedDevices`): stopped, or with it in their row's `leftCategories`. Turning sync on again rejoins: the directory row without the stop goes first, and no category starts until iCloud has it; then each category forgets its engine state and other Macs' coordination rows (`rejoinIfPending`), then sends every row as new. Fencing purges against a rejoin is DEV-1149. A stop or rejoin counts as sent only once its row, read from iCloud itself (`CloudAccess.recordValues`), says so: CloudKit can refuse the record alone, as over quota, while sending succeeds, and SQLiteData's copy of the server record then holds what was sent.
- Turning Profiles and spaces or Tab Archive off is a leave (DEV-1148, `SyncCategory.leavesWhenTurnedOff`): saved (`SyncSettings.leftCategories`), then sent to the directory once the engine went quiet, confirmed and retried as a stop is (`unpublishedLeaves`), and the other Macs' purges of that category leave this Mac out. Turning it on again rejoins without resetting the engine, so edits from both sides merge by their conflict metadata: the directory row without the leave goes first, then the category's database is copied to `arx-icloud-sync-rejoins/`, the engine fetches everything, and `KeptRows` brings back from the copy what purges deleted meanwhile that this Mac didn't delete itself (`SyncSettings.pendingMerges`), with the open tabs and space and folder state a fetch's cascade took, which triggers keep in the attached copy as they go. No repair runs on the category until then, and the app hears of its batches once, merged. A fetch that fails, or turning the category off or pausing sync before the merge, brings that back at once and drops the copy, so the next attempt copies the rows again; a copy left by quitting mid-rejoin is merged the same way at the next launch. A rejoin iCloud doesn't have yet holds back only its own category.
- `SyncSettings` is this Mac's own state in `arx-icloud-sync.json`; it never syncs. Every toggle is a per-Mac choice (`SyncToggle`): a category's here, a Preferences group's in the preferences file, a profile's in the local organization file.
- `WorkspaceSyncClient` is Settings' dependency. Its live value is `unavailable`; the app installs `.live(sync)` when it makes a `WorkspaceSync`.
- Merging what matches (DEV-1216), behind `ARX_ICLOUD_SYNC_MERGE` (`ICloudSyncMergeFeatureFlag`, on by default). See "Merging what matches" below.

There are no Tabs from other devices yet. Delete from iCloud stays in `WorkspaceSync` but isn't offered in Settings, since decision 1 rules out a deletion that empties other Macs: "Stop syncing this Mac" replaces it.

## A refused upload (DEV-1207)

On 18 November 2026, turning sync on in a Release Arx deleted every space, folder, pinned tab and uploaded Favorite on that Mac. The Release build uses the container's Production environment. Its saves all failed, most likely because Arx's schema had never been deployed there. CloudKit answered every record whose parent is a profile or space with `referenceViolation`, since the parent wasn't saved. SQLiteData 1.13 takes that error to mean "the parent was deleted on the server": it deletes the row here as a user deletion, so the cascade takes the folders, pins and open tabs, and queues every deletion for iCloud. Three things now guard against that:

- Only a fetch or Arx deletes a synced row that has a parent (`SyncedDeletions`, in WorkspaceDatabase). The library's delete is skipped, the row stays, and nothing is queued.
- At every start, each category queues again the rows iCloud never saved (`ServerConfirmation.requeueUnconfirmed`). The library writes a row's `lastKnownServerRecord` when it builds a batch, before sending, so `hasLastKnownServerRecord` doesn't mean iCloud has the row; only a saved record has a change tag. A save refused with most errors (`invalidArguments`, `serverRejectedRequest`, `limitExceeded`…) is dropped and never retried, so a refused first upload is sent again at the next launch. A row with no last known record at all counts as unsaved too: a save refused with `serverRejectedRequest` makes the library forget the row's record. The repair pass reads which profiles iCloud has the same way (`ServerConfirmation.confirmedKeys`).
- The rows a start queued again are watched until iCloud saves them (DEV-1209). A temporary trigger on the category's writer connection, which the engine writes through too, notes each row whose last known record the library writes (as it builds a batch) or clears (after a `serverRejectedRequest`); it is dropped once no row is watched. Whenever the engine isn't sending, a watched row that a send tried and iCloud still hasn't saved was refused: the category's status becomes `CategoryStatus.refused(count)`, which Settings › iCloud Sync shows under the category and in the status line. This doesn't depend on seeing `isSendingChanges` go on and off, which a short send can do between two looks. A save CloudKit retries by itself (no network, a busy zone) counts until it succeeds. The next start queues the rows again and starts over; rows written after a start are watched from the next one.
- Before a category's first sync, its database is copied to `arx-icloud-sync-backups/` (`<date>-first-sync-<category>.sqlite`). Without the copy, the category doesn't start.

**Production schema.** Release builds use the Production environment, which only has the record types and fields deployed from Development in the CloudKit Console. Deploy the schema before a Release build turns sync on, and again whenever a synced table or column is added. Without it, every save fails and nothing syncs; with these guards nothing is deleted, and Settings shows the categories as failing. The schema the code needs is `CloudKit/arx.ckdb` at the repository's root; `CloudKitSchemaTests` fails when a synced table or column isn't in it, and `make cloudkit-schema` writes it again (DEVELOPMENT.md › iCloud schema).

## Wiring it into the app

- Each category's rows must be written through the `DatabaseWriter` its engine was made on: the engine tracks changes with temporary triggers on that one connection. Phase 1's store writes preferences through an `ATTACH` on the organization connection, which the preferences engine can't see, so preference writes need to go through the preferences file's own connection when sync is on. Both connections then need a busy timeout, since they share the preferences file in rollback-journal mode.
- `remoteChanges()` yields a category after an engine changed its rows and the repair pass ran: the store must reload from the database then, or its diff writer keeps writing against rows it no longer has.
- Onboarding's "Sync with iCloud" (DEV-1215) uses two calls. `accountIsAvailable()` says whether the account can be read now. `joinFromOnboarding(turningSyncOn:)` turns sync on (`setEnabled(true)`, or, when it's already on, starts what didn't start as an account change does, for Try again), then fetches each category that's on once and runs its repair pass, yielding `OnboardingSyncEvent.fetching` for each, and ends with `finished(OnboardingSyncResult)`: whether iCloud's organization has a profile with a space, and the space any Mac used last from the synced `usage` rows. Sync turns on before the account is read, so a request made while iCloud is briefly out of reach starts once it's back. It yields `unavailable` without an account or when a category is paused or held by an account review, and `failed` with the text of a save or fetch error. Cancelling the stream stops only the waiting: sync stays on and goes on fetching. With `turningSyncOn: false`, onboarding's background wait after Continue without waiting, it never turns sync on and yields `turnedOff` when sync is off. The app keeps its first-launch Default profile "This Mac only" while this runs, so the first upload doesn't carry it and `starterProfileIDs` is empty; after Continue without waiting the app keeps it local until this answers, because DEV-1216's merge on joining runs only before Profiles and spaces first starts.
- `WorkspaceRepair.Summary.duplicateStarterProfiles` are this Mac's starter profiles that the first merge found identical to one from iCloud. Their spaces, copies of the kept profile's, are removed and the profile stops being a starter, so a later Mac's first merge never keeps it; the profile row and its website data stay. `WorkspaceSync` keeps their IDs in `SyncSettings.duplicateStarterProfileIDs` (device-local) until the app has offered to remove them (`WorkspaceSyncClient.duplicateStarterProfiles` / `forgetDuplicateStarterProfile`, DEV-989).

## Merging what matches (DEV-1216)

Turning sync on for a Mac that already has its own workspace merges what belongs together instead of copying everything. `WorkspaceMerge` is the pure part: `Snapshot` (this Mac's synced rows as it shows them, or iCloud's from `CloudAccess.zoneRecords`), `match` and the preview `Plan`.

- **Profiles** match by name, ignoring case and spaces (`nameKey`); of several candidates the pair whose spaces overlap most (names, then pin addresses) wins. "This Mac only" profiles and what this Mac hid never take part.
- **Spaces**, within a matched profile: the starter space with the starter space, then by name, then by pins in common: at least two, and more than half of the smaller space's (`isStrongOverlap`).
- **Folders** match by their path of names, **pins and Favorites** by address (`addressKey`: scheme and host lowercased, trailing slash and fragment ignored). A match becomes one item; this Mac's others go after iCloud's in each folder, in this Mac's order.
- **iCloud wins** for a matched item's settings, and for a matched pin's or Favorite's own title; the preview lists the differences, which need review. Unmatched profiles and spaces are added as they are, a taken name with the Mac's name: "Work (MacBook Air)" (`uniqueName`).

**The joining Mac** (Profiles and spaces never started here) merges before anything uploads. `startCategories` reads iCloud's organization zone before any category starts (`joinMergeAllowsStart`). A plan that needs review (`Plan.needsReview`: anything added, any setting from iCloud, any new pin, folder or Favorite, a match by pins) is published as `WorkspaceSyncStatus.mergePreview` (`.joining`) and every category waits, `.paused(.mergeReview)`. One that doesn't, such as an untouched starter into iCloud's, goes ahead. Offline, nothing starts and the account retry tries again. `confirmMerge(keepingSeparate:)` reads iCloud again and, after the first-sync backup, matches this Mac's rows in the same transaction that writes the merge, so a save since the preview is merged too. A plan that differs from the one approved (or, without a preview, one that now needs review) is shown again instead, with nothing written. `WorkspaceMerge.applyJoining` writes iCloud's copy of each kept item and of every folder a kept pin or folder is in (with iCloud's values: when they upload, CloudKit's `serverRecordChanged` merges them into the same values), moves this Mac's open tabs, selection, folder and tab state and the IDs in its JSON rows to the kept items, moves the rest in, and deletes the merged rows, which never reached iCloud. It records each replaced item in `mergeRemap`, so a save the app makes from the workspace it read before the merge lands on the kept items (WorkspaceDatabase README). `cancelMerge()` turns sync off with nothing sent. Only the joining Mac merges, so two Macs can't merge each other at once. The flag off, the first merge is the old one: everything is copied and only an untouched starter is folded in (`dropDuplicateStarterProfiles`).

**Website data stays.** A merged profile's cookies and storage stay where they are: `profileStorage` (WorkspaceDatabase, never synced) makes the kept profile's ID an alias for the merged one's storage on this Mac, never a copy. The workspace store publishes the aliases to `BrowserCore.ProfileStorageAliases` at every load, and the engines name a profile's storage through them: `ChromiumBrowserEngine`'s `chromiumProfile(legacy:)` and `Profile.ID.chromium(_:legacy:)`, and WebKit's `dataStoreIdentifier(for:)`. History merges into the kept profile's through the app's history store (`BrowserHistoryClient.moveProfile`, one entry per page; `WorkspaceSync`'s `moveHistory`), then a `.history` change has the app show it. Tab Archive entries keep their rows and only their profile and space change, also in later archive repairs for entries that arrive after the merge. The history mirror's rows of the merged profile stay as they are.

**Already-synced duplicates**, such as two Macs' Defaults from before this, are listed in `WorkspaceSyncStatus.duplicateProfiles`. `previewProfileMerge` shows the same preview (`.profiles`) for two of them at a time, and shows it again instead of merging when it changed by the time the person confirms; the first in the switcher stays, and a third is offered again after, against what the first merge made. `WorkspaceMerge.applySynced` moves the unmatched items into it and writes a synced `mergedItem` row for each matched item. Every Mac's repair pass applies them (`applyMergedItems`), following a merge into an item merged since to the last kept one (`mergedTargets`; two Macs merging the same items in opposite directions at once end in the lowest ID): what's still in a merged item moves to the kept one, the Mac's own open tabs, selection and state follow, its history moves, and the merged item is hidden as this Mac's deletion (DEV-980). A kept profile this Mac never used (no alias, open tab or `usage` row; a space the merge moved, which a `movedSpace` row names with the profile it left, counts for that one, wherever the fetch already put it) becomes an alias for the merged one's website data when this Mac used that one, read before anything moves; one it used keeps its own, so no page open in it changes storage. Once every Mac has hidden it, the purge deletes it everywhere.

Preferences don't take part yet: they merge per column as before (DEV-1219). History that another Mac mirrors after a merge (DEV-1217) and downloads through a second merge (DEV-1218) are follow-ups too.

## What SQLiteData 1.13 can't do

- **Keep other Macs' rows when a zone is deleted.** A Mac that sees a zone deleted deletes every row it got from it, with no delegate hook. `deleteFromICloud` keeps this Mac's rows by resetting its own engine state (`resetEngineState`, which depends on the `sqlitedata_icloud` metadatabase layout), but other Macs lose theirs, so Settings doesn't offer it (ADR 0001, decision 1).
- **Scope an engine's fetches to its zone.** Every engine fetches the whole private database, so each also stores the other category's records in its metadata as an unknown record type and re-reads them on every fetch. Harmless at this size; a fork could implement `nextFetchChangesOptions`.
- **Hold uploads before an account decision.** On sign-in the library queues this Mac's unsynced rows before the delegate runs; the delegate stops the engines at once, so nothing is sent until the person decides.
- **Repair in the same transaction** as the fetched batch, or know a fetch succeeded. It runs after the engine stops fetching, and checks placements with CloudKit before moving anything.
- **Cancel a fetch in flight.** `stop()` drops the engine without waiting, so a fetch can still write afterwards. Replace waits up to 30 seconds for a stopped engine to go quiet before clearing, and fails (keeping the review) if it doesn't.
- **Let the later binding win.** Per-column times live in the library's metadata, so duplicate keyboard bindings keep the command first by `(position, id)` instead.

## Tests

`WorkspaceSyncTests` run "Macs" on SQLiteData's mock container. Its engine starts each time with no change token, so a paused category refetches its whole zone on resume, which CKSyncEngine doesn't; pausing with unsent local deletions needs the two-Mac check (ADR 0001, validation 7).

## Deletions stay local (DEV-980)

ADR 0001's decision 1: deleting a profile, space, folder, pinned tab or Favorite on one Mac doesn't delete it on the others. With sync's separate connections, the store's diff writer writes a synced `deletion` row (item, table, this Mac's `StoreInfoKey.deviceID`, date) instead of deleting the record, and the store's reader leaves out what this Mac deleted (`OrganizationRows.hiding`). Writing an item again, as an undo does, removes this Mac's deletion row. The diff writer also records use in synced `usage` rows, at most once a day per item and Mac, or again when the item was deleted since: an edited item, a space whose selected tab changed and that tab, an expanded folder, and the profile of a used space or Favorite. Opening a space, pinned tab or Favorite records use too, even when no row changes (`WorkspaceDatabase.recordUse(of:)`, called by AppFeature's `recordingUse` as each comes on screen, at most once a day). Rows only this Mac has (open tabs, selection, expansion) are deleted directly.

**Delete from this Mac only, or remove from iCloud (DEV-1336).** Removing a synced profile or space while another Mac syncs Profiles and spaces (`WorkspaceSyncStatus.asksWhereToRemove(now:)`: a Mac in the directory, not this one, seen in the last 30 days, that hasn't stopped or left the category) asks which, "Delete from This Mac Only" first. Neither deletes anything on another Mac at once; both write this Mac's `deletion` row. "Delete from This Mac Only" sets its `keepsElsewhere` (the app calls `WorkspaceDatabase.keepRemovalOnThisMac(_:)` before the save, which marks the rows of the item and everything in it): "sync deletions after" never counts that deletion, so it goes only when every Mac deleted the item, and no Mac marks it. "Remove from iCloud" is the deletion as it always was. Nothing goes from a Mac without being marked there first: `WorkspaceRepair.deletionMarks` lists every profile and space another Mac removed from iCloud that this Mac still shows, with the first such Mac, and, with "sync deletions after" on here and nobody having used it since, the day the purge takes it (`deletesAt`; `nil` means it stays until the person removes it). `WorkspaceSyncStatus.deletionMarks` publishes them, read again after each organization repair, `refreshStatus()`, a change of the setting and a Keep; a "this Mac only" profile's synced copy is left out. Removing a marked item here is an ordinary deletion, which counts towards every Mac having deleted it. `keep(_:)` (`WorkspaceRepair.keep`) turns every Mac's removal of the item and everything in it into a deletion from that Mac only, through the organization's connection, so the mark clears on every Mac and the purge never takes it, while the Macs that removed it still hide it. A deletion of a folder, pin or Favorite, one made with no other Mac syncing, and an agent's deletion are removals from iCloud as before. "Sync deletions after" is a per-Mac setting whose purge deletes everywhere, so each Mac publishes it in its directory row (`syncDeletionsAfterDays`, DEV-1337), and a purge by time, like a mark's date, goes ahead only when every counted Mac that still has the item (one that didn't delete it) has it on (`DeletionPolicy.devicesSyncingDeletions`, this Mac's own setting taken as it is now). A Mac with it off keeps the item whatever the removing Mac's setting, and so does a Mac whose row doesn't say, as an older Arx's. Without a device directory the policy counts every Mac as on, as before.

The repair pass deletes an item for good, which the library then deletes everywhere, when every Mac in `syncDevice` seen in the last 30 days deleted it (deletions count per table, since a tab keeps its ID as a pin and as a Favorite), or with "sync deletions after" on (`SyncSettings.syncDeletionsAfterDays`, off by default, 30 days when on, Settings › iCloud Sync) when the first deletion is older than that and no Mac used it since. Each Mac writes its `syncDevice` row at most once a day from the organization's repair. A purge can't be undone, and a fetch can stop before a newer use, an undo, another Mac or a pin added to a deleted space arrived, so `WorkspaceRepair.purgeCandidates` runs twice: on this Mac's rows and on iCloud's, read from the whole zone with only the fields it needs (`CloudAccess.zoneRecords`, `PurgeSnapshot`). An item goes only when both say so, and only when everything deleting it here would delete is in it in iCloud too, so a pin another Mac moved out of a deleted space isn't deleted with it; a record iCloud can't read stops the check. After iCloud disagreed, the zone is read again once the candidates change or an hour passed. A purged folder's children that weren't deleted move up to its parent. While the organization syncs, the repair runs again every six hours, since time alone can make a deletion go, and a repair whose lookups saw the "sync deletions after" setting change waits for the next pass. The repair also leaves a pin or Favorite beside a tab this Mac has open, which it unpinned, in iCloud and hides it here, instead of deleting it as a duplicate: another Mac may have made it a Favorite since.
