# PageCaptureFeature

Capturing on the page itself. Depends on BrowserCore, BrowserCaptureClient, BrowserPageInspectionClient, AppUI and TCA. It owns no browser session or native engine.

- **Capture Area…** (`mode: .area`) dims the page and shows a pill: "Click or drag to capture a portion of this page". The whole page dims until something is in focus, which is left clear. The pointer is a "+" crosshair with a camera badge (`NSCursor.pageCapture`, laid out by `PageCaptureCursorLayout` on whole points so it stays crisp at 1× and 2×; the hot spot is the crossing). Hovering outlines the innermost block under the pointer, from the engine's node bounds; a click captures it. Dragging draws a free-form area. The arrow keys move the highlight (←/→ previous and next block, ↑ the enclosing block, ↓ the first block inside) and Return captures it.
- **Capture Page…** (`mode: .page`; the site menu's and the developer header's Capture) captures the whole page straight away. An engine with DEV-1172's whole-page capture renders it beyond the viewport, as DevTools' full size screenshot does (`request.beyondViewport`, `PageCaptureLimits.wholePage`): frames, video and canvas included, and a page taller than 16384 pixels is cut there, with "Too long to capture whole. Cut at N points." in the menu (`cutPoints`). Capture Full Page takes it too. When the whole page fails that way (unsupported, as with DevTools open, unavailable or over its limits), or on an engine without it, it's the engine's recorded full-document capture, within its limits (8192 pixels a side and 16M pixels, at one pixel per layout pixel, so about 4096 points tall on a Retina display). The engine can't cut a page short, so a page that's too long, or that has content it can't capture whole (frames, canvas, video, filters, animation), gets its visible area instead, and the menu says why (`visibleAreaReason`). A busy capture, a timed-out one (whose work holds the engine's capture slot until it drains) or a changed page fails as before, and the next pick is cut from the viewport.
- **Beyond the viewport** (`capturesBeyondViewport`, the `ARX_CAPTURE_BEYOND_VIEWPORT` feature flag, DEV-1208, on by default). Capture Page's frame shows the whole page it took: the frozen image, scaled down to fit the viewport with a 24-point inset and centered, outlined, with the menu beside it (`PageCaptureGeometry.capturedFrame`). A page that fits the viewport keeps the viewport's frame. In Capture Area, dragging toward an edge scrolls the page (`autoscrollAvailable`: the flag, DEV-1172's whole page and the engine's scrolling). The scroll starts 36 points inside an edge the pointer moved toward, at most a quarter of the viewport. Its speed grows with the square of the depth, from 60 to 3000 points a second, reached 160 points past the edge zone (`autoscrollVelocity`). A 16 ms timer sends one scroll at a time (`PageCaptureDrawing`), making up for ticks spent waiting on the last one. Each scroll reports how far the page actually moved, so the area grows by the real scroll and stops at the page's end. Letting go while a scroll is out takes the area once it's back. The area runs from where the drag began, moved with the page, to the pointer held inside the viewport (`region(from:to:scrolled:viewport:)`). An area that's all on screen is cut from the viewport as before. A larger one is cut from a whole page captured beyond the viewport, at that capture's own scroll and scale (`documentRegion`), and its frame is the image scaled to fit like a whole page's. Past where a long page was cut, the capture fails as over its limits. A scroll that fails turns scrolling off for the session. One that never ran (busy, a page magnified with a pinch, or a `file:` page the engine runs no scripts in) leaves the page where it was, and drawing goes on within the viewport. Any other failure, such as a timeout, may have moved the page anyway: the area drawn so far is dropped, the pill says "Couldn't follow the page as it scrolled. Draw the area again.", and the rest of that drag, its release included, does nothing; the next drag draws within the viewport. Return and a click on a block don't take one mid-drag or while an area waits on its last scroll, and a capture stops the scrolling. While the pointer is down, or an area waits on its last scroll, the wheel doesn't scroll the page, so the area counts every scroll. Off, the frame wraps the viewport and an area stops at its edge.
- **The visible area** (`mode: .visible`), for device capture and a chat's capture, is captured straight away.
- After capturing, the frame stays on the page with a menu beside it: Copy (⌘C), Save… (PNG), Edit (opens the PNG in Preview), Send to Chat when the host allows it (`allowsSendingToChat`: an ordinary window's page), Retake, and Capture Full Page for a `.visible` capture when the engine supports full-document capture. Copy, Save, Edit and Send to Chat end the capture; canceling the Save panel keeps it. Send to Chat reports `delegate(.sendToChat(image, title:))`; the host opens the window's chat and attaches the PNG (DEV-798).
- **Device capture** (`framing`, DEV-757), from the developer header's popover with the `ARX_DEVICE_CAPTURE` feature flag on: a visible-area capture whose image `BrowserCaptureClient.frame` puts in a device on a gradient. In Portrait Mode it first crops a phone-shaped slice of the viewport the frame was captured at (`DeviceCaptureStyle.portraitRegion`). Capture Full Page frames the whole document as a window. Copy, Save, Edit and Send to Chat take the framed image.
- **From the chat** (`destination: .chat`), set by the host for the chat's Capture Visible Area and Capture Area…: the capture goes to the chat as soon as it's taken, without the menu.
- Escape, a click away from the frame, switching tabs or closing the window ends the capture.
- **Sound feedback** (DEV-1256, `SoundFeedbackClient`): Copy plays `capture` once the image is on the clipboard and Save once it's in its file, since nothing on screen shows either; Edit and Send to Chat stay silent, as the result is in sight. A capture, copy or save that fails plays `couldNot`, except a page that changed under the overlay while it's retaken (`maximumStaleRetries`). The client decides whether anything is heard.

`PageCaptureOverlay` is native: SwiftUI drawing plus an AppKit view that takes the pointer and keyboard, placed above the page's surface through `BrowserPageOverlay` (BrowserSurfaceClient). Nothing is injected into the page, so pages can't read or style it. Scrolling passes through to the page; node bounds are viewport points, so they're dropped while scrolling and read again once it settles.

The clear cut-out is `PageCaptureCutoutShape`, whose bounds are animatable: it springs between hovered blocks, follows a drag with a quicker spring, and shrinks into the last block's center when nothing is in focus (`PageCaptureGeometry.cutout`). Reduce Motion turns the animation off.

`PageCaptureGeometry` holds the pure selection geometry: which nodes can be picked (no inline text, nothing tiny, nothing covering the whole viewport), hit-testing, drag regions, arrow-key movement, the cut-out and its corner radius, and where the menu goes. It also holds the beyond-viewport math (DEV-1208): drawn areas after a scroll, the scroll speed, the area's place in a whole page, and the captured frame. Rectangles are viewport points with a top-left origin.

The host (AppFeature's `CaptureSessions`) owns the session list, resolves a fresh session revision and inspection source, and sends `prepared`. `delegate(.retake)` asks it for a fresh revision of the same page; `delegate(.dismiss)` ends the session. Removing the session cancels its capture, inspection and export effects. Pixels and node bounds are read only after the host's live `PageCaptureAuthorizationClient` check, before and after awaiting the engine. Node bounds are read in 128-node windows, at most 2,048 nodes, and the inspection scope is revoked after each read. Pages without bounds (private windows, non-HTTP(S) documents) still allow drawing an area.

Crops use `PageCaptureFrame.pixels(in:)`, so a selection in points becomes device pixels at the capture's scale (Retina captures keep 2× pixels). `PageCaptureFrozenImage` decodes a capture at the size its frame shows it, so a whole page scaled into the frame doesn't decode all of its pixels. The protected-media note appears only when the engine warned about it and a media block inside the capture came out blank (`PageCaptureCodec.isBlank`).

Interface text uses AppUI's generated catalog accessors.
